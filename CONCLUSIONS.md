## What we did

1. Codex processed 177 long 2026 videos of youtube.com/@daily-ecommerce; 138 were in scope (auto-captions only, so names and numbers can be garbled).
2. From all 138 we built a pool of 31 sellable physical products, after excluding ingestibles, drug or medical-device claims, pseudoscience, digital products and saturated electronics.
3. We checked each against public data:
   - Meta Ad Library, US, active ads, grouped by landing domain and by ad age (90+ and 180+ days);
   - site traffic Jun-Sep 2026 (SimilarWeb via adlibraryspy.com);
   - brand and category search (Google Trends);
   - prices, Alibaba costs, China duty, regulation;
   - AOV and repeat cycle, giving a rough LTV.

## Where our method can be wrong

### It may drop good options (false negatives)

1. **Traffic undercount.** SimilarWeb is weak under ~50K visits. Stores also split traffic across landers, advertorial domains and checkout domains. "SMALL" may be wrong for MLMT and Skult, so check with a second source before closing them for good.
2. **Channel blind spot.** We only see Meta ads and the brand's own site. Products that sell mainly on TikTok Shop or Amazon (several videos were about TikTok Shop) are invisible to us and look "small".
3. **Season.** Seasonal products (ski sleeve, tanning) were judged off-season.
4. **Brand search is naturally low** for impulse DTC products, so a low value does not mean no demand.
5. **Hard regulatory exclusions** (LED masks, EPA repellents, FDA-listed garments) may hide good niches that have a compliant route; they need more capital and paperwork, not a ban.
6. **One source.** Everything comes from one YouTuber's picks; good products outside his channel were never considered.

### It may keep bad options (false positives)

1. **Ads and traffic ≠ profit.** Refunds, chargebacks and real margins are unknown. Menvault's product fails its own claims in a user test; Trustpilot complaints exist for several leaders.
2. **Guru crowding.** Products shown on the channel attract his students. The soaps are already cloned, so we would enter late as CPMs rise.
3. **Assumptions.** Conversion 2-3% and a 35% repeat share are guesses; LTV and revenue bands are only order-of-magnitude.
4. **Geography.** Kaori's traffic is only 34% US.
5. **Short window.** One snapshot (Sep traffic, today's ads). A September spike can be one viral moment.
6. **Not yet checked:** sample quality, landed cost, real US CPM/CPA, and whether we can match the leaders' creative volume.

### Mistakes made and fixed along the way

- Codex's top 3 was taken over at first without our own check.
- Longevity was first measured as "the oldest ad in the top 30"; the 180+ day filter replaced it.
- The massager search hit unrelated advertisers.
- The lash-serum row was based on a magnetic-lash brand.
- Price assumptions were taken from the YouTuber's clones instead of the real stores.
- Ad counts were trusted without checking traffic.
