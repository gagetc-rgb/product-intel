How we judge a product

Every hit follows the same path: one seller breaks out, clones arrive and spread to other channels, the market fills up, then fades. We read where a product is on that path from public signals, instead of blending everything into one score.

Q1

Demand

How many units move per month, summed over Amazon + TikTok Shop + own stores?

category level
Q2

Proof

Do people who see the real numbers keep paying for ads and keep getting buyers for months?

leader brands
Q3

Stage

Where on the life cycle, and how crowded relative to demand? Is an angle still open?

category + leaders
GATE

Fit

Can we sell it: rules, margin, AOV/LTV, logistics, creatives? Pass or fail, not a score.

us

Marks: ◆ confirmed · ◈ likely (one decent or vendor source) · ◇ heuristic / our assumption. AOV = average order value, LTV = lifetime value of a customer.

2 · Your question

A product is not on TikTok. Is it weaker?

Only if three things are all true. Otherwise the gap is neutral: "not yet" or "not suited".

Selling visibly for 6+ months?
No → neutral: clones haven't had time to arrive.
→
Does the channel suit it?
No → neutral. TikTok: 5-second demo, impulse price. Amazon: people search for it. Meta + own store: needs a story, higher AOV. ◇
→
Does the channel allow the category?
No → neutral. Some health and medical items are restricted on TikTok Shop and Amazon.
→
Then the gap counts against it
Demand is narrower than it looks: it depends on one brand's ads or one creator wave. ◈
~65%of 37 brands saw Amazon search rise in the same month their TikTok Shop sales grew. ◈ The Bluebird Group, 2025
48.8% vs 9.5%prefer to buy on Amazon vs TikTok Shop after discovering on social. ◇ one survey (Envision Horizons, via Modern Retail)
Order is not fixedReal hits spread across channels, but some start on Amazon or TikTok. So we count channels as a stage signal, not a demand signal.
3 · Q1 Demand

One currency: units per month, summed across channels

Every volume signal is turned into orders a month with an error band. Signals that cannot be turned into volume are used for proof or stage only.

Amazon "X+ bought in past month"
Floor of unique buyers in 30 days; sum on-target top-20. ◈ Within one bucket of real sales >90% of the time (PMG). Needs 50+ buyers.

Proof needs two independent signals that agree

  • Ads 90–180+ days, many variants, relaunches ◈
  • Review curve rising or stable for 6+ months ◆ (Earthly Derm and Everstride matched their traffic)
  • Traffic / Amazon rank history without a cliff for 3–6+ months ◈
  • Search compounding over months, not a 6–8 week spike ◈

Traps: brand-awareness and retargeting ads run long without selling much ◈; reviews can be gated or imported ◆.

5 · Q3 Stage + crowding

Crowded is relative: sellers vs demand, and how fast they arrive

  • Demand per seller: Amazon units ÷ listings selling 1K+; TikTok revenue per shop ◈ (Kalodata uses it)
  • Concentration: top-3 sellers' share. Spread out = easier to enter
  • Entry speed: share of advertisers whose ads are all under 30 days ◇
  • Price war: seller count up while price falls ◈ (Keepa reading guides)
  • Same hook everywhere = the obvious angle is burned ◇

Open angle test (ours, ◈)

Inside one category, compare active ads with ads aged 180+ per angle. Compression socks: wide calf and nurses survive (proven); men, plus size, pregnancy are tried but rarely survive (open but unproven).

Limits

What this method still can't see

  • Free Amazon and TikTok data are snapshots. Direction over time needs Keepa (Amazon history) or FastMoss / Kalodata (TikTok history), both paid. ◆
  • Meta never shows spend; logged-off Ad Library loads only ~30 top ads per query. ◆
  • Conversion factors (2–3% site conversion, own-store review rate) are guesses until checked on one store with known sales. ◇
  • Saturation and demand thresholds come from vendor blogs, not research. ◇
