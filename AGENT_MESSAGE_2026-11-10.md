## 3. What to check more than once

One rule: a number or a fact goes on the site only if you opened the source yourself and can link to it.

1. **"Nobody sells it", "0", "100%", "empty shelf".** The most dangerous claims. Before writing one, search in the local language: the country's Amazon, two local marketplaces, the Meta library, a plain Google search. Write down what you found. Your "the German shelf for shower filters is completely empty" fails on the first search: Stone Stream and MineralStream sell them in German.
2. **Names of companies, agencies, brands.** Each needs a link. We could not find "Shoalvex agency" anywhere. No link, remove it.
3. **Platform rules and laws.** Primary source only: the regulator's or the platform's own page. Read to the end: Amazon says mouth tape is banned under any other name too.
4. **The search phrase.** Open the top 20 results and count the on-target share. Under 80%: change the phrase or filter. Record the share and the date next to the number (section 9.5).
5. **Brand age.** Listing age is not brand age. Check the brand's site, the domain date, the first Wayback capture. You already can do this: you did it right for adult diapers.
6. **Google Trends.** Three pulls on different days, the mean, two control terms. Many zero weeks = the term is too rare, do not use the number.
7. **Country splits.** Two independent sources. The Meta library shows no spend and no US reach at all (section 9.6). A third-party tool's figure is an estimate ◇; name the tool.
8. **Review counts, ratings, sold counters.** Reopen the listing before hand-in and record the date. You have 1,400 reviews for PawZ on Chewy; we saw 636.
9. **Arithmetic.** Recompute every sum and share. Today these do not add up: 38 products vs 32 in the summary, "12 schemes" vs 6, "7 sellers" with 5 named, "58 country cuts" vs 63 in the cards.
10. **Card vs shortlist.** The same number must be the same everywhere. Today prices and costs differ between the cards and the shortlist.
11. **Verdict vs kill rules.** Before each green verdict open section 6 of the brief and check the "captured" and "small demand" rules. Dog boots: 62% held by a 2008 brand is "captured".

## 5. Do not

- Invent numbers or names. No data = "not measured" and the reason.
- Write "enter" without measured demand, concentration and newcomers in the target market. An honest "not proven" beats a nice green verdict.
- Recommend ways around platform rules, patents or laws. That is a risk, not an opportunity.
- Bypass captchas or bot checks. If blocked, stop and write a manual collection task.
- Use numbers from videos anywhere except the "what was claimed" block.
- Blend everything into one score.
- Use hype wording ("bloody war", "drags them through the courts", "a stunning thing"). This is research, not an ad.
