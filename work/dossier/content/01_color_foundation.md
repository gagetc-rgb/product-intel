# Color-changing foundation

**Verdict: #1 of 20.** Every layer we measure points the same way. This is a young market that is still growing fast, and the money in it already goes mostly to brands that did not exist on Amazon a year ago. ◆

## 1. The product and the buyer

A foundation, usually a stick or a light liquid, that comes out of the tube in one neutral shade and "adapts" to the skin tone as it is blended. The pitch on every leading listing is the same: *one shade for everyone, made for mature skin*. Luxe sells it as "for Mature Skin", Prime Prometics as "for Mature Women", Prime Prometics' oldest live Meta ads say "Color-Changing Foundation For 50+ Skin". The buyer is a woman over 50 who is tired of shade-matching and of foundation settling into lines. ◆ (listing titles and ad texts)

Typical price on Amazon: $21.99 median, half of all listings between $16.99 and $26.99. The three new leaders sell above that: Luxe at $27, Prime Prometics at $44, Smooche at $49. ◆

## 2. How big, and where it is going

**Size.** The first five pages of Amazon search for "color changing foundation" show at least **322,950 purchases a month** (sum of the "bought in past month" badges). At the listing prices that is at least **$10.0 million a month** on these five pages alone. ◆ count, ◈ money (the badge is a floor and counts buyers, not units; discounts and multi-packs are ignored).

**Trend.** US Google searches for "color changing foundation" over the last 12 weeks are **4.7 times** what they were in the same weeks a year ago, after correcting for a Google-wide spike in spring 2026. Yearly averages tell the same story: 0.6 → 0.8 → 2.4 → 5.3 → 26.7 (index, 2021-22 to 2025-26). The category went from almost nothing to a mass product in about 18 months. ◆ (one pull of three; pulls 2-3 pending)

> Chart: 5-year weekly search curve. The spike in April-June 2026 appears on control terms (shampoo, body lotion) in the same weeks, so it is a Google data artifact, not demand.

## 4. Can a newcomer get in

Yes, and this is the strongest case of the 20. **Three brands under 24 months old sell 1,000+ a month each**, and 23 unbranded listings do too. The unbranded part (90,050 units) is where the price war is: sellers like Facelove, LATORICE and MilkySeoul each sell about 10,000 a month at $29-45, without a known brand name. ◆

What this means for us: the brand route (own name, own store, Meta ads, Amazon as a second channel) is proven three times in 10 months. The cheap-Amazon route is crowded with unbranded sellers already. ◈

## 6. Do the winners keep growing

Not measured yet. Monthly review counts per store are the planned proxy (task for the owner: `work/blind/REVIEW_TASK_GPT.md`). What we can say: all three leaders entered 9-10 months ago and are still at 20,000-150,000 a month, so none has faded yet. ◈

## 7. What the winners say

The oldest live ads (471 days, Prime Prometics) open with "Color-Changing Foundation For 50+ Skin — Tired of…". The angle is age plus frustration with ordinary foundation, not beauty in general. Smooche's 404-day ads lead with an offer ("BUY 1, GET 1 FREE"). ◆ (`work/ads/longrunning_v2.json`, images saved)

## 8. Risks and open questions

- **Quality gap.** Leader ratings of 3.4-4.0 suggest a lot of disappointed buyers; returns and reviews may hurt a newcomer as fast as they help. ◈
- **The peak may be near.** Search grew ×4.7 in a year; such curves often flatten. Watch pulls 2-3 and the next months. ◈
- **Brand-age dating is estimated** from ASINs, not from "Date First Available" (not collected). ◈
- **Advertising cost** is unknown; 12,000 active ads mean a crowded auction. ◈

## 9. Verdict and what would change it

**#1.** Pre-registered kill rules: demand is not falling (×4.66), new brands take 211,800 units/mo with many shops keeping 90+ day ads. It would drop if search interest fell below ×0.8 of last year, or if the new-brand share collapsed in a later scan.
