## Insoles: is it a medical device (primary sources, 2026-11-11)

- ◆ 21 CFR 890.3475 "Limb orthosis": a device "worn on the upper or lower extremities to support, to correct, or to prevent deformities or to align body structures", examples include "a corrective shoe". Class I, exempt from 510(k) and most QMS rules (ecfr.gov).
- ◆ FDA annual establishment registration fee: $11,423 for FY 2026, **$13,785 for FY 2027** (Federal Register 2025-14412; fda.gov MDUFA fees page).
- ◈ Reading: insoles sold for comfort, standing or work with no medical claim are a consumer product. Wording like "orthopedic", "corrects flat feet", "relieves plantar fasciitis" makes them a medical device (class I, exempt): no premarket review, but establishment registration + device listing, labeling rules and complaint files. Who pays the fee in a dropship/private-label setup (the factory, a specification developer, the initial importer) is not checked; see FDA "Who Must Register, List and Pay the Fee". Practical rule for a copy: write comfort and support claims, not treatment claims.
