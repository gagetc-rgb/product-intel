## 4. Regulation

**Leak-proof boxers**
- ◆ 21 CFR 876.5920: a "protective garment for incontinence" is a Class I device, exempt from 510(k) and most GMP ([eCFR](https://ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-H/part-876/subpart-F/section-876.5920)).
- ◆ The initial importer (and a specification developer) must still register the establishment and pay the annual fee: **$11,423 for FY2026** ([Federal Register](https://federalregister.gov/documents/2025/07/30/2025-14412/medical-device-user-fee-rates-for-fiscal-year-2026); [guide](https://meddeviceguide.com/blog/fda-establishment-registration-device-listing-guide)). The foreign maker must register and list too.
- ◇ Whether incontinence-claimed boxers fall under this depends on intended use and claims. Get a regulatory consultant's opinion before importing.
- Cheaper route: buy from an already-registered US importer for the test.

**Meta ads**
- ◆ Meta's personal-attributes policy bans ads that assert or imply the viewer's physical or medical condition ([Meta](https://transparency.meta.com/en-gb/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/personal-attributes)). Hooks like "you leak when you cough" must be rewritten in the first person or as product statements. Competitors' first-person stories are how they stay within the policy.

**Bracelet**
- If we use CZ stones, never call them moissanite. Disclose the plating and base metal (FTC Jewelry Guides).

- ◆ The de minimis exemption is suspended, so every shipment pays duty.

## Conclusion

1. **Tennis bracelet: GO to a test.** It has the biggest real search demand, the lowest landed cost, no special regulation and the best margin. Risks: extreme competition ($15 Amazon floor) and peak Q4 ad prices. Plan: buy samples now (tarnish test), build assets in Dec, launch for Valentine's in mid-Jan with an image-first test.
2. **Leak-proof boxers: GO only through a cheaper route.** The need is real and US-specific, the AOV is high and customers rebuy. But importing under our own brand probably costs $11.4K/yr in FDA fees, and the leader's product is publicly shown to underperform its claims. Option: run a demand test with a US-registered supplier's stock, or after a regulatory opinion. Our angle: honest, lab-tested absorbency.
3. **Ski sleeve: NO-GO for buying stock this season.** Nobody searches for the problem, enthusiasts dismiss it, ski visits dropped 15% last winter, and the window is short. Optional: a $300-500 pre-order/waitlist ad test in December to measure interest without inventory.
