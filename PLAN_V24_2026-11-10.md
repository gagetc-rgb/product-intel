## 3. Idea: "How the top changed" — an animated ranking history

Status: idea, fixed here. Not to be built now.

### 3.1 What it shows

One screen. The products are tiles in a ranked column. A timeline at the bottom has one stop per piece of evidence. Stepping forward, tiles slide up and down to their new places; some fall out, some enter. Next to the column a short card says what we learned at this step and who moved because of it.

The point for the viewer: the first list came from the videos alone, and almost every new source of data reshuffled it. So a ranking is only as good as the evidence behind it, and the decision to enter must wait for the test.

### 3.3 Data file (delegated)

`work/site/ranking_history.json`: for each step — id, date, title, one-sentence "what we learned", the source file, and an ordered list of products with rank or group. Rules:
- Compile only from the files named above. A product that a step did not rank is "not in the list", never interpolated.
- The product set changed between steps (31 candidates, then 124, then 17, then 20). Track the current 20 plus the notable drop-outs (tennis bracelet, ski sleeve, leak-proof boxers, massager, seat cushion, duffel). Say on the page that the pool itself changed.
- Steps 10-11 have groups, not places. The animation must show that as a change of form (the numbered column dissolves into three bands), not fake a ranking.
- Every step links to its source file so a reader can check it.

### 3.4 How to build it (delegated, after the storyboard)

- One self-contained HTML page in the site's own style and fonts; inline SVG or absolutely positioned tiles; no chart library needed.
- Movement: FLIP technique (measure old and new positions, animate the transform), 500-700 ms, eased; tiles that leave fade and slide off; new ones slide in.
- Controls: previous / next buttons, a clickable timeline, arrow keys, autoplay with pause. A tile click pins that product and draws its path across all steps.
- Each tile: photo, name, a small mark of why it moved at this step.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`: no movement, the steps switch instantly. Below the animation a plain table with the same data.
- Phone: one column, the caption card above the tiles, swipe between steps.
- Check: every rank on screen equals the data file; no console errors; works without network.

### 3.5 Reserved for Fable

- The storyboard: which steps to merge so the story has 7-8 beats and not 12.
- The caption of each step (one plain sentence, in Russian) and the closing line.
- How "places dissolve into groups" looks at step 10.
- Final review of motion and pacing.

Honesty note for the storyboard: the early steps were not errors corrected by better data only. Three times we changed the rules after seeing results (noted in `RUNBOOK.md` and `METRICS.md`). The page should say so; it is part of why the test, not the ranking, decides.
